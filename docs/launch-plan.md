# Getting Squish! found (written 2026-10-24, the day it went live)

Free app, no ad budget, kids' category. Discovery comes from four places, in order of
how much they actually move a small app: the store page itself, parents telling parents,
the kids-app review sites that parents trust, and (later) Google Play's Teacher Approved
badge. Everything below is free and kid-safe.

## Week 1 — make the page work
1. **Get the first reviews.** The store page with zero ratings is invisible in search
   ranking. Ask 5–10 friends/family with a Fire tablet to install it from the Appstore and
   leave an honest rating. Do not ask for 5 stars, do not review it yourself, do not use
   anyone with the developer account's email. Amazon suppresses apps that game reviews.
2. **Check the live page** on a phone and on a Fire: title, first screenshot, first 200
   characters of the short description (that's all Fire shows before "read more"). If
   the promo image isn't shown, that's normal: Amazon uses it only in curated placements.
3. **Keywords are now in Amazon's search index.** Search the Appstore for "slime",
   "sensory", "no ads kids" from the tablet and see where it lands. Note the position; it
   is the number to beat.

## Weeks 1–4 — parents telling parents (the only channel that really works at this size)
4. **One honest post** where parents of young kids already are: r/FireTablet, r/Parenting
   (Thursday "what apps" threads), r/toddlers, r/Autism_Parenting (the "no timers, no
   scores, calm sensory" angle is genuinely useful there), Facebook groups for Fire Kids
   tablets and for sensory/OT parents. Format that works: "I'm a dad, I built a slime app
   for my kids because every one on the store had ads. It's free, no ads, no purchases,
   here's what it does, here's the link, feedback welcome." Reply to every comment. One
   post per community, never cross-post the same day, read each sub's self-promo rule
   first (some allow it on specific days only).
5. **Ask the OT crowd.** Occupational therapists and SEN teachers recommend apps to dozens
   of families each. Email three or four local OT practices or the therapist your kids'
   school uses: short note, the no-ads/no-pressure pitch, the link. Offer nothing; the
   app being free is the offer.
6. **The kids are the demo.** A 20-second phone video of a child molding slime on the Fire
   (hands only, no face) is the single best asset for every post above. Film it once.

## Month 1–2 — the review sites
7. **Common Sense Media** (commonsensemedia.org) accepts submissions from developers via
   their "submit a title" form; a review there is the biggest trust signal a kids' app can
   get. **Children's Technology Review** and **Smart Apps for Kids** also take submissions.
   All three want: the store link, a one-paragraph pitch, screenshots, and a promo code
   (not needed, it's free). Pitch the research angle: made because the category is
   ad-funded; no data; calm sensory play.
8. **Amazon's own levers.** In the developer console: Promotions → "Developer Select" and
   seasonal promotions. Amazon periodically features new kids' apps; a complete, clean
   listing with a promo image and 10 screenshots is what their merchandisers pick from.
   There is no form for Amazon Kids+; a well-rated kids app is how you get invited.

## Month 2+ — Google Play (the bigger pond)
9. Google Play's Kids tab is driven entirely by the **Teacher Approved** badge, and we are
   close to an ideal candidate. It needs the $25 account, ID check, 12 testers × 14 days,
   then opt in to Teacher Approved on day one of production. Budget: an hour of forms plus
   the two-week test clock. Everything else is already built (AAB, listing, privacy page).

## Keep doing
- Reply to every store review, especially the bad ones, within a day. Parents read the
  developer replies more than the reviews.
- Ship a small update every month or two (a new theme, a new stamp). Each update
  re-triggers "recently updated" and is a reason to post again.
- Track in the console: downloads/week, rating, which countries. If a country with a
  non-English Fire market shows up, the app has no text, so a localized title and
  description is a 10-minute job.

## Don't
- Don't buy installs or reviews. In the kids category this gets apps removed.
- Don't add ads to "grow faster". It destroys the one thing the app has over the field.
- Don't spam. One good post per community, once.

## Honest expectation
A clean kids app with a few dozen honest reviews and a Common Sense listing typically
settles at a few hundred to a few thousand installs a month on Amazon. Google Play with
the badge is where it could go further.
